\section{Automated Scientific Hypotheses \& Proposed Protocols}
Derived from identified empirical contradictions, our system generates falsifiable hypotheses to resolve literature divergence:

\subsection*{HYP-001: Frequency-Decoupled Dynamic Attention for Extreme Sensor-Noise Mitigation}
\noindent\textbf{Hypothesis Statement:} Under extreme darkness (< 0.1 lux), decoupling high-frequency Poisson-Gaussian sensor noise from low-frequency structural contours prior to self-attention eliminates the 8.6\% mAP regression on DarkFace while preserving global relational context on ExDark.

\noindent\textbf{Tension Addressed:} Contradiction between DarkFace (where raw ViTs suffer from severe noise amplification) and ExDark (where ViTs outperform CNNs under moderate urban night lighting). \\
\noindent\textbf{Theoretical Rationale:} Standard global self-attention computes dot-product affinities across all spatial tokens indiscriminately. In extreme low-light, dark current and photon shot noise dominate token variances, causing cross-token attention to collapse into high-entropy noise patterns rather than semantic edges. \\
\noindent\textbf{Proposed Protocol:} Implement a dual-branch Wavelet-Gated Transformer (WG-ViT). Branch 1 processes low-frequency sub-bands with global attention; Branch 2 processes high-frequency bands with localized bilateral filtering. Benchmark on DarkFace (extreme dark) and ExDark (urban night) against standard ViT-B/16 and Swin-T.

\noindent\textbf{Testable Predictions:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{WG-ViT will improve mAP@0.5 by >= +7.5\% over vanilla ViT-B on the DarkFace benchmark.}
    \item \textit{Performance regression on ExDark will remain below 0.3\% mAP.}
    \item \textit{Computational overhead will remain < 12\% additional FLOPs relative to baseline ViT.}
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Falsification Criteria:} If wavelet-guided token separation yields less than +1.5\% mAP improvement on DarkFace compared to standard spatial patch embedding, the hypothesis is falsified. \\
\noindent\textbf{Feasibility Tier:} \texttt{High} \quad \textbf{Novelty:} \texttt{Breakthrough} \quad \textbf{Impact Score:} 92\%

\subsection*{HYP-002: Luminance-Aware Hybrid Preprocessing Gating Mechanism}
\noindent\textbf{Hypothesis Statement:} An adaptive illumination gating network that bypasses neural enhancement filters when ambient lux exceeds 1.0 lux resolves the negative transfer artifact (-0.4\% mAP) observed on natural low-light images.

\noindent\textbf{Tension Addressed:} Empirical conflict where low-light enhancement algorithms improve DarkFace detection by +8.2\% mAP but cause regression on ExDark due to hallucinated color and contrast over-amplification. \\
\noindent\textbf{Theoretical Rationale:} Enhancement models (e.g. Zero-DCE, EnlightenGAN) are optimized for human perceptual luminance rather than downstream object detection feature invariance. Under moderate lighting, aggressive tone mapping distorts discriminative boundary gradients. \\
\noindent\textbf{Proposed Protocol:} Train an end-to-end differentiable lux estimator with soft routing between direct sensor pass-through and multi-scale Retinex feature enhancement before feeding into detection backbones.

\noindent\textbf{Testable Predictions:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Adaptive gating will achieve Pareto-optimal mAP across both DarkFace (>46.0\%) and ExDark (>78.0\%).}
    \item \textit{Elimination of over-saturation artifacts in urban night street scenes.}
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Falsification Criteria:} If a single static enhancement model matches or exceeds adaptive routing on both datasets simultaneously, the gating hypothesis is invalidated. \\
\noindent\textbf{Feasibility Tier:} \texttt{High} \quad \textbf{Novelty:} \texttt{High} \quad \textbf{Impact Score:} 88\%
